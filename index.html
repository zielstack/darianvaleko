Personal Trajectory

From Systems Engineering to Financial Systems

1. Engineering and Systems Background

Born into a family of engineers in Singapore, his formative years coincided with Asia’s industrial integration into global manufacturing. Early on, he recognized that what truly determines outcomes is not isolated efficiency, but the structure of the entire system.

Enrolling at the National University of Singapore (NUS) in 1988, he majored in Electrical Engineering and Systems Control with a minor in Statistics and Decision Science. Rather than individual technological breakthroughs, his research concentrated on failure pathways within complex systems, risk amplification mechanisms under multiple interacting variables, and long-term stability versus short-term optimization.

2. Institutional Finance and Asset Allocation

Transitioning into financial infrastructure rather than conventional technology, he joined the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) in 1992 in research and market surveillance—analyzing interbank settlement risk, foreign exchange clearing, and institutional contagion. There, he developed his core thesis: collapses originate not from price fluctuations, but from breakdowns in settlement, trust, and liquidity.

In 1996, he moved to the asset-liability management (ALM) division of a major European commercial bank in Switzerland, managing interest rate curves, maturity mismatches, and balance sheet solvency. Subsequent decades in Switzerland and Boston focused on multi-horizon allocation models for pension funds, insurance capital hedging, and foundation balance sheets.

3. Digital Assets as a Structural Layer, Not Speculation

Approaching digital assets through systemic implications rather than price speculation, his focus centered on decentralized settlement eliminating clearing risks, non-sovereign liquidity rails, and smart contracts reducing institutional friction.

Beginning in 2014, he established multi-asset digital risk models, custody protocols, and compliance frameworks for institutions, anchored by one unwavering principle: If an asset cannot be incorporated into a risk framework, it does not deserve a place in the portfolio.

Core Philosophy

Structural Integrity Matters More Than Returns

Internal investment committee doctrine governing risk, capital survivability, and systemic stress.

Investment Principles
01

Risk precedes return.

Risk is not volatility; it is an irreversible mistake. Capital must be insulated before it can participate in growth.

02

Capital must survive before it compounds.

A well-constructed portfolio is one that can continue to exist even through the most difficult years.

03

Volatility is not risk; fragility is.

Price fluctuations represent market liquidity variance; structural fragility causes permanent loss of capital.

04

Structure matters more than timing.

The purpose of capital is to endure market cycles, not simply to chase short-term market peaks.

How We Think • Methodology
Methodology I

Across Cycles

Portfolios are structured to endure multi-decade macro transitions, inflation regimes, and structural realignments. We avoid rotating investment fads and short-term forecasting in favor of balance sheet durability.

Methodology II

Under Stress

Rigorous stress-testing of liquidity thresholds, non-linear correlation cascades, and counterparty contagion. If the market were to fail, we design portfolio structures so that failure pathways are pre-insulated.
